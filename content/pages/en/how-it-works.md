## 1. You tell us who you know

A short form, about ten minutes: names, dates and places for you, your parents and your grandparents. Leave blank whatever you do not know. Blanks are not a problem; finding them is what the research is for.

You can also say what you most want to learn, such as where a surname came from, or anything at all about one great-grandparent nobody talks about.

## 2. A person does the research

Teresa works through parish books, civil registers and censuses herself. Nothing is matched automatically and your family is never compared with anyone else's tree. You can follow the status of your request at any time.

How long it takes depends on the place and the period. Some lines open up in days; others depend on an archive answering a letter.

## 3. You read what was found

The findings are published privately for you:

- a written account of what was found, what is still open, and the sources for each claim
- a family tree drawn as a star chart
- a map of where each person was born, lived and died
- a birth report for every ancestor with a full birth date: a natal chart and a written reading
- the original documents, where they could be copied or linked

You can invite relatives to read with a private link. They see the findings but cannot change anything, and living people appear by relationship only.

## What it costs

Ascendant is **free during its first version**. In return we ask for your patience and, when your findings are published, your honest opinion.

When prices are introduced they will be shown here before you submit anything, and requests already submitted will be finished at no charge.

## What we do not promise

- **No instant results.** There is no button that returns a tree in thirty seconds.
- **No invented ancestors.** If a record cannot be found, the findings say so.
- **No guessed birth hours.** Most ancestors' birth hours were never written down. Their reports show what can honestly be known from the date alone.
