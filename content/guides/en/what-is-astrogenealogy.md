Genealogy tells you who your ancestors were: names, dates, places, trades. Astrology offers a portrait of a person from the sky on the day they were born. Astrogenealogy puts the two side by side, one birth chart for every person in the tree.

## Why put them together

A family tree is mostly empty of character. You may know that your great-grandmother was born in 1902 and died in 1989, and nothing about what she was like. A birth chart will not tell you that as fact. What it gives you is a portrait to hold up against the little you do know: the stories, the one photograph, the trade she chose.

Many people find that this is the moment an ancestor stops being a line in a register.

## What people look for

- **Repeated signs.** The same Sun or Moon sign turning up in a grandmother, a mother and a daughter.
- **Balance of elements.** A family heavy in earth signs that kept the same farm for two centuries, or a restless line full of fire and air.
- **Comparisons.** Laying two charts over each other, a couple or a parent and child, to see where they met easily and where they rubbed.

## What it cannot do

It cannot replace records. A chart is only as good as the birth date behind it, and that date has to come from a document. It cannot prove that a trait is inherited, and it should never fill a gap in the tree with a guess.

It also has less to work with than a modern chart. Most ancestors have no recorded birth hour, so their charts show the planets by sign, without a rising sign or houses.

## How to start

1. Write down what you know: yourself, your parents, your grandparents.
2. Find the full birth dates. Certificates, family books and parish entries all help.
3. Cast a chart for each person and read them next to one another.
